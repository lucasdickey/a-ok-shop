# Proposed next prompt: prepare A-OK for one physical prototype

Prepare the technical and visual collateral needed for a professional service to manufacture, finish, assemble, and ship one A-OK “Apes on Keys” figurine-keychain prototype for my review.

Use the supplied original character reference and the accompanying draft vendor brief. Treat the existing long Codex/Blender brief as design input, not proof that any model, validation, or approval already exists. The concept specification image is reference art, not a validated engineering drawing.

## Work sequence

1. Inventory the actual source files and modeling tools. Clearly distinguish existing assets from requested deliverables. Do not present generated pictures as printable geometry.
2. Resolve the scale conflict before committing final geometry: the original brief says 101.6 mm from soles to top of cap excluding the eyelet; the concept sheet says 100 mm including the eyelet. Record the owner's choice. Confirm whether the sample should be painted. Keep budget, schedule, and shipping destination as open commercial inputs until supplied.
3. Assess whether available tools can produce a faithful sculpt. Choose the modeling workflow for resemblance and manufacturability; do not make procedural generation a prerequisite. If faithful modeling is not feasible, deliver a complete sculpting commission brief and identify the remaining modeling work. Do not substitute a generic ape or label a primitive blockout print-ready.
4. Build an editable sculpt preserving expression, proportions, clothing, graphics, and visible details. Use primary art for appearance; log disagreements among views and resolve significant ones through an actual-model preview. Keep the integrated eyelet connected to the head and metal hardware separate. Save previews during refinement.
5. Obtain approval of the actual model views before final print preparation. Use the chosen fabricator's material and geometry guidance to resolve thicknesses, attachment details, supported fine features, weight, hollowing/drainage if needed, and support placement. Document changes that affect appearance.
6. Export the selected fabricator's accepted print format. Include a millimeter-scaled STL when accepted, and the editable native master. Provide OBJ/MTL plus texture images or a color-capable 3MF only when needed and supported. Reopen exported files independently to inspect scale, geometry, and color. STL has no reliable embedded unit or color specification; state units and dimensions in the handoff. Do not fabricate file extensions or claim export compatibility without verification.
7. Create a dimension sheet from the final model, model renders, finish map, hardware specification, and inspection report. Record actual bounding dimensions, triangle count, watertight/manifold results, degenerate geometry, normals, self-intersections, disconnected components, structural connections, minimum relevant thicknesses, and eyelet clearance. Mark each check as measured, visually inspected, vendor-reviewed, or not yet verified. Mesh checks do not establish physical strength or finished color accuracy.
8. Package the files with a concise README and draft quote request covering sculpting if outstanding, one finished print, hardware assembly, packaging, and shipping. Ask for named material, itemized cost, achievable tolerances, lead time, revision allowance, physical sample checks, and editable-model handoff terms. Do not contact vendors or place orders unless I explicitly authorize it.

## Minimum deliverables

- Editable native model and verified vendor-compatible print geometry, **if modeling was successfully completed**.
- Color/texture files only as required by the selected color-print workflow; a finish map and reference images for a painted workflow.
- Actual-model approval views, final dimension sheet, eyelet detail, and geometry inspection report, **only when derived from a real model**.
- Vendor brief, file manifest with revision identifiers, and a list of unresolved requirements or missing assets.
- Draft request for one prototype including finishing, hardware, packing, and delivery.

A reproducible build script is optional. STEP, extra interchange formats, batch quotes, mold tooling, retail packaging, and production-run planning must not delay the first sample.

## Completion labels

Use the label that the evidence supports:

- **Ready for sculpt-and-prototype quotation:** references, requirements, and scope are clear enough for a shop to quote modeling and fabrication; printable geometry may still be absent.
- **Ready for fabrication review:** actual exports, dimensions, finish instructions, and inspection evidence exist; selected shop still needs to confirm acceptance.
- **Released for one prototype:** owner approved the model, shop confirmed process/material/price/lead time, and owner authorized the order.
- **Physical prototype received for review:** the finished, assembled sample has been delivered. Digital files alone do not satisfy this milestone.

Do not claim a milestone that has not occurred. Preserve a clear distinction between artwork, digital model validation, vendor acceptance, and physical sample testing.
