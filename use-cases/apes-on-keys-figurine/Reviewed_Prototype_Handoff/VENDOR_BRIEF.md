# A-OK “Apes on Keys” — one prototype quotation brief

Revision: draft 01 · 26 September 2026

**For quoting and design discussion. Not released for manufacture.**

## Requested result

Please quote one full-volume figurine keychain, modeled from the supplied character art, printed, finished, fitted with metal keyring hardware, and shipped for physical review. We currently have reference images and a written brief; we do not yet have a printable model. Please confirm whether you can provide the character sculpt as part of the job, or identify the modeling deliverables you need supplied.

Preferred quoting route: detailed resin print with hand-painted finish. Name the proposed resin, print process, primer/paint/protective finish, and any limitations for handling as a keychain. A direct full-color alternative may be quoted separately with its material, finish, and file requirements. These are quoting options, not approved process selections.

## Attachment, geometry, and fabrication review

Use an integrated reinforced eyelet at the top of the cap/head, structurally tied into the head mass with rounded transitions. Keep loose metal hardware separate from printed geometry. Quote a 25–30 mm metal split ring and short chain or jump-ring connection, including assembly. Specify ring/chain dimensions and material/finish, verify insertion clearance and articulation, and check how the assembled figure hangs. A lobster clasp is optional, not required.

The original modeling targets are 1.5 mm minimum structural thickness and 2 mm or more for exposed/load-bearing details. Treat these as preliminary design targets requiring your material/process review, not a guarantee of strength. Reinforce the brim and headphone connections without changing their visible identity. Model drawstrings, shoelaces, and fine details as supported relief where necessary. Preserve legible cap and hoodie graphics; propose paint/decal or raised-relief treatment with a close-up preview.

Please propose solid versus hollow construction and estimate finished weight. If hollowed, document wall thickness, drain/vent access, cleaning and curing access, and any closures; avoid trapped uncured resin. Keep support scars and seams away from the face and primary graphics where practical. State any changes needed before printing. Soles should allow the figure to stand for inspection.

## Deliverables and approval sequence

1. **Quote:** Separate sculpting and revision costs, printing, finishing/painting, hardware, assembly, packing, shipping, taxes, and any setup charges. State included revision rounds, lead time, payment milestones, and the remedy for damage in transit. Destination/postal code and desired date remain to be supplied.
2. **Digital model approval:** Provide front, rear, both sides, top/bottom as useful, three-quarter, and eyelet close-up views rendered from the actual sculpt. Include dimensions and an uncolored view. Obtain owner approval of resemblance and dimensions before fabrication.
3. **Print handoff:** Supply or confirm the vendor-accepted geometry file, units in millimeters, final bounds, mesh inspection results, material/finish instructions, and hardware specification. Include color files and all texture assets only when the selected process uses them. Provide the editable master and final exported model to the owner; state reuse rights and any handoff restrictions in the quote.
4. **Physical sample:** Print, clean/cure, finish, paint if selected, and assemble. Record actual dimensions and weight. Check visible defects, lettering, standing/hanging behavior, and hardware fit. Agree a material-appropriate handling/attachment check before fabrication; report the method and result. Do not describe routine keychain durability as validated unless it has been assessed.
5. **Shipment:** Provide photos of the finished sample, protect the brim/headphones/eyelet and paint in packaging, ship to the owner's confirmed address, and provide tracking.

The owner will review the delivered sample's likeness, scale, finish, legibility, feel, weight, and attachment. This order is for one prototype only. Future batches and molding are outside the initial scope.
